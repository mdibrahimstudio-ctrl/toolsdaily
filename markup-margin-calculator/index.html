FAQ

What is the difference between markup and margin?

Markup is the profit as a percentage of cost — how much you add on top of what you paid. Margin is the profit as a percentage of the selling price — how much of each sale you keep. The same sale can be a 50% markup and a 33.33% margin: buy for $40, sell for $60, and the $20 profit is 50% of the $40 cost but only 33.33% of the $60 price.

How do I price for a 40% margin?

Divide your cost by 1 minus the margin as a decimal. For a 40% margin, divide the cost by 0.6 — so a $40 cost needs a selling price of $40 / 0.6 = $66.67. Enter your cost and target margin above and the calculator works out the price for you.

Can margin be over 100%?

No. Margin is profit divided by the selling price, and profit can never be more than the full selling price — even if you got the item for free, the profit equals the price, which is a 100% margin. Markup, on the other hand, has no upper limit: a $1 cost sold for $101 is a 10,000% markup but only a 99.01% margin.
