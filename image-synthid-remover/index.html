    FAQ

    What metadata hides inside a photo?

    Photos can carry EXIF data: the camera or phone model, the date and time, and often GPS coordinates of exactly where the photo was taken. Files may also hold XMP editing data, PNG text chunks (some AI image tools store the prompt there) and C2PA content credentials that state how the image was made.

    Does removing metadata change the picture itself?

    No. This tool removes the embedded metadata and AI-source tags attached to the file — EXIF, GPS, camera info, XMP and C2PA — by re-drawing the picture into a fresh file. The pixels you see are not edited, cropped or filtered; only the hidden data attached to the file is left behind. One honest limit: pixel-level watermarks are embedded in the image itself, not in metadata, so they cannot be removed this way. Saving as JPG applies normal JPEG compression, exactly like any photo editor.

    Why remove metadata before sharing?

    A photo posted online can reveal your home or workplace location (from its GPS tag), your phone model and when the picture was taken. Removing the metadata first is a simple privacy step before posting to social media, marketplaces or forums.

    Is my photo uploaded anywhere?

    No. The scan and the cleaning both run entirely in your browser — your photo never leaves your device.
